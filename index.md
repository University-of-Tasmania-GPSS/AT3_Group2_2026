## Two glaciers on a changing island

Heard Island is a remote volcanic island in the southern Indian Ocean, located near 53°S, 73.5°E. Brown and Stephenson lie on the eastern side of the island ([](#fig-study-area)), where published inventories record particularly substantial glacier retreat (Tielidze et al., 2025). Their proximity makes them a useful comparison, while their different shapes and terminus histories provide distinct settings to investigate.

### Why investigate this change?

Tielidze et al. (2025) documented widespread glacier area loss on Heard Island between 1947 and 2019, with an especially large front retreat at Stephenson. Their interpretation highlights glacier shape and lagoon development as possible influences on the contrasting retreat histories. These provide a starting point for examining the spatial evidence in this project.

A glacier can lose area, retreat inland and become thinner at different rates. **Area loss** measures the reduction in its mapped footprint; **front retreat** measures displacement of its seaward edge. We compare both, then examine the shape of the lower glacier, surrounding terrain, mapped surface materials and the water-filled basins exposed by retreat. Bringing these observations together helps assess which interpretations are supported and which still require direct measurements.

(research-question)=
## Research question

**How do the mapped retreat histories of Brown and Stephenson glaciers differ, and what can glacier geometry, mapped geology, flow and archived field evidence tell us about that contrast?**

### Objectives

1. **Measure change:** compare mapped glacier area and front position across the available inventory dates.
2. **Compare spatial setting:** examine lower-glacier geometry, sub-ice terrain, mapped geological units, ice velocity and a geometric surface-exposure index.
3. **Evaluate field evidence:** compare archived Brown Glacier radar sections with modelled bed estimates and reconstruct lagoon depths within the area supported by soundings.
4. **Integrate and communicate:** bring the evidence into a comparative interpretation and interactive map, while identifying unresolved mechanisms and the observations needed to test them.

:::{dropdown} Scope of the evidence

The comparison uses glacier outlines for **1947, 1988, 2014, 2019 and 2026**. The supporting evidence spans different periods: the RADARSAT 2002 surface DEM includes 1997 input, the ice-thickness model represents approximately 2010–2020, retained velocity observations cover 2016–2022, and the archived radar and lagoon surveys date from 2000 and 2004. These layers describe different parts of the glaciers' histories and are not a simultaneous survey.

Brown and Stephenson are our focus. Other glacier outlines provide island-wide context, with recent non-focus outlines treated as provisional. Interpretation also follows on from the limits of each dataset: mapped Snow/Ice does not identify buried lithological units, modelled bed elevations remain estimates, and lagoon depths are reconstructed only within supported survey coverage. The [Methods](methods.md) explain how these limits shape the analysis.
:::
